# A-Team Hiring Worksheet (expanded)

**Source:** The Founder's Field Manual, Chapter 5 — Hiring Your First Ten
**Agent use:** When a subscriber is about to make a hire (first teammate through team of twenty), is drowning in work they shouldn't be doing, or has just had a hire go wrong. Hire for alignment, not just skills.

## Step 1 — The highest-leverage role
What is slowing you down right now that someone else could do better or faster? What must you stop doing yourself? Complete: "I need a ____ who can ____, so I can focus on ____."

*Agent check:* the "so I can focus on" clause is the test. If the founder can't name what they'd reclaim, the hire is headcount theater. Also challenge title inflation — the first ten rarely need a "VP."

## Step 2 — Define success, not just skills
Three outcomes for the first 90 days, plus three soft traits that matter as much. Both lists specific enough that a candidate could read them and self-assess honestly.

*Agent test per outcome:* could two people disagree about whether it was achieved? If yes, add a number or an artifact.

## Step 3 — Red flags for this role
From past hires: 2–3 behaviors that would be red flags (avoids ownership; talks more than ships; strong opinions, poor listening). For each, plan the interview question that surfaces it — red flags don't volunteer themselves.

*Question patterns the agent can offer:* "Tell me about a project that failed — walk me through your part" (ownership); "What did you ship in your last 90 days, personally?" (talker vs. shipper); "Tell me about a time you were convinced and wrong" (listening).

## Step 4 — Culture alignment check
The three unwritten values every new hire must reflect. Cross-reference the fire-for test in T1-ch04: interview for the values you'd actually fire over.

## Sequencing guidance (early-team patterns)
The first ten hires set the ceiling: every early hire recruits the next ones in their own image. Rules of thumb the agent can apply: hire doers who can stretch into managers, not managers who once did; generalists before specialists until the workload proves the specialty; never delegate sales entirely before the founder can close repeatably themselves.

## Common failure patterns
1. **Hiring for the company you hope to be** — a scaling-stage exec into a validation-stage company (see the big-company failure mode in T3-hard-things-field-notes.md).
2. **Mirror hiring** — ten people who all think like the founder; comfortable, and blind in the same spots.
3. **Skills-only scorecards** — the résumé passes, the 90-day outcomes never get written, and "fit" gets judged on vibes at exit interview.
4. **Slow firing** — knowing at week six, acting at month eight. The team always knows first.

## Output the agent should produce
A one-page role scorecard: the leverage sentence, 90-day outcomes, soft traits, red flags with paired interview questions, and the culture checks — ready to use in interviews. (Full interview kit: PB-hiring-scorecard-playbook.md.)
