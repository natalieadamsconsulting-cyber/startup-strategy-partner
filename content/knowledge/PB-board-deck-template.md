# Playbook: Board Deck & Investor Update Template

**Tier 1 — original playbook.** Extends Ch 9 (funding relationships) and Ch 16 (metrics). Prerequisite: the T1-ch16 worksheet — the deck reports the *real* dashboard, not a flattering parallel one.
**Agent use:** When a subscriber has a board meeting or investor update due. Principle: the deck is a decision-forcing document, not a performance. Send it 72 hours ahead; spend the meeting on the two or three decisions, never on reading slides aloud.

## Board deck structure (10–14 slides, quarterly rhythm)
1. **CEO summary (1 slide):** three things that went well, three that didn't, the one thing keeping the founder up at night. Candor here sets the meeting's honesty level — see "tell it like it is" in T3-hard-things-field-notes.md.
2. **Scorecard (1–2):** the Metrics Pyramid — North Star, the 3–5 KPIs, each vs. plan and vs. last quarter. Same metrics every quarter; swapping metrics quarter to quarter is the #1 credibility destroyer.
3. **Financial snapshot (1–2):** cash, monthly burn, runway in months, revenue vs. plan. Runway stated to the month, with the assumption set named.
4. **Deep dive (2–3):** one topic per meeting (pricing change, channel economics, org design) — decided by what decision the board must help make.
5. **Asks and decisions (1):** specific, assigned, dated. A board meeting without asks was an expensive status email.
6. **People (1):** key hires, departures, org risks.
7. **Appendix:** the operational detail; anything a director might ask about, pre-answered.

## The monthly investor update (for investors not on the board)
Five sections, under 500 words: **Numbers** (same 4–6 every month) / **Good** / **Bad** (always include one real item — updates with no bad news stop being read) / **Asks** (one or two, specific: "an intro to a payments-compliance hire") / **Thanks** (who actually helped, by name — it trains helping).

## Cadence discipline
Monthly updates even when things are ugly — *especially* then. Investors reference-check founders on communication behavior in bad quarters; the silent-when-struggling pattern kills bridge rounds and follow-ons that the numbers alone wouldn't have. Missing two updates in a row telegraphs trouble louder than any bad metric.

## Agent watch-fors
- **Two-dashboard syndrome:** board metrics diverging from the operating dashboard (T1-ch16's metric-theater failure). One truth, everywhere.
- **Runway optimism:** runway quoted assuming unclosed revenue; state the bear case.
- **The 40-slide deck:** volume as a substitute for a point of view.
- **Surprise management:** bad news delivered live in the meeting. Directors should never learn a material negative in the room — call them before.
- **No asks:** a board used only for governance is an unused asset; a specific ask per meeting per director is a reasonable bar.

## Output the agent should produce
A filled deck outline with the founder's actual numbers, the CEO summary drafted in their voice, the asks list, and — if metrics differ from the T1-ch16 dashboard — a flag on every divergence.
