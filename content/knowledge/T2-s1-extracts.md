# Tier 2 — S-1 Extract Briefs (public domain source: SEC EDGAR)

**Purpose:** Structured briefs from real IPO filings so the agent can ground metric and strategy discussions in disclosed data rather than folklore. All figures from the companies' S-1 registration statements (public filings). Format: business model → key disclosed metrics → what founders should notice. Two briefs here; roadmap Phase 2 targets 8–10 (candidates: Figma, Klaviyo, Instacart, HashiCorp, Coinbase, Warby Parker, Toast).

---

## Airbnb (S-1 filed November 2020)
**Model:** Two-sided marketplace; revenue = service fees on gross booking value (GBV).
**Disclosed metrics worth teaching from:**
- 2019 (last pre-COVID year): $38.0B GBV, $4.8B revenue (~13% effective take rate), 247M guest arrivals
- Net loss in every year disclosed, widening with growth investments — profitability was a choice deferred, then forced: COVID cut revenue ~30% in 2020 and triggered the restructuring in CS-public-case-briefs.md #1
- ~91% of traffic arrived organic/direct — the moat was brand + network, not paid acquisition; sales & marketing spend was cut drastically in 2020 with modest booking impact, proving it
- Risk factors: regulatory exposure city by city (a Ch 13 lesson at global scale), host supply as the binding constraint
**Founders should notice:** take rate arithmetic (GBV vs. revenue confusion inflates many pitch decks); organic acquisition as the difference between a marketplace that compounds and one that rents growth; the S-1's cohort disclosures as the gold standard for proving retention (T1-ch11).

## DoorDash (S-1 filed November 2020)
**Model:** Three-sided logistics marketplace (consumers, merchants, Dashers); revenue from commissions + consumer fees; DashPass subscription layered on.
**Disclosed metrics worth teaching from:**
- Growth: $291M (2018) → $885M (2019) → $1.9B (revenue, first 9 months of 2020) — category leadership seized from a later-entrant position against Grubhub/Uber Eats
- Marketplace GOV cohorts disclosed: each annual cohort spent more in subsequent years — the plateau curve the flywheel worksheet demands (T1-ch11), at filing scale
- Contribution margin turned positive only in 2020; unit economics arrived *with* scale and COVID tailwinds, not before — a candid example of the leverage test (T1-ch07) resolving late
- Suburban-first strategy in the S-1's own strategy section: winning less-contested geographies before dense cities — a beachhead argument (T3-crossing-the-chasm) in a filed document
**Founders should notice:** you can be late and win on segment selection + execution; cohort spend curves are how sophisticated buyers of the story (public investors) evaluate retention claims; three-sided models multiply both network effects and cash burn.
