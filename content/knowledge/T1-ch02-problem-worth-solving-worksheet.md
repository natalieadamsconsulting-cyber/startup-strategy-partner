# Problem Worth Ten Years Worksheet (expanded)

**Source:** The Founder's Field Manual, Chapter 2 — Finding a Problem Worth Solving
**Agent use:** When a subscriber has a candidate problem to pressure-test. Not a brainstorming tool — the discipline is testing what they already think, not manufacturing optimism. ~90 minutes.

## Step 1 — Write the Duct-Tape Observation
Pick one specific person, by name, who has the problem. In one paragraph, describe how they duct-tape it today: tools, workarounds, staff time, silent cost. **Rule:** if you cannot write the paragraph from observation, you have not found the problem yet — go watch first.

*Agent check:* the paragraph must contain at least two concrete tools/workarounds and one quantifiable cost. "They struggle with spreadsheets" fails; "the office manager exports from three systems every Friday and reconciles for four hours" passes.

## Step 2 — Map to the Pain Triangle
Three legs: **lose time, lose money, feel anxiety.** One leg = nice-to-have. Two = a business. Three = a company that almost builds itself. Only one honest leg → the problem is too thin.

*Agent probe per leg:* Time — hours per week, whose hours, at what loaded cost? Money — direct spend, error costs, missed revenue? Anxiety — what keeps them up; who gets fired if this goes wrong?

## Step 3 — Run the 10-question problem audit
Walk the chapter's audit in writing, marking green/red flags. **More than three reds → the problem is not ready, or the founder isn't.** The agent should record the tally and specific reds for the 30-day follow-up.

## Step 4 — Narrow to a niche
Write the customer description three different ways: specific company size, specific role, specific trigger event. Pick the narrowest version that still contains enough customers to matter. Cross-reference: beachhead criteria in T3-crossing-the-chasm-field-notes.md.

## Contrarian test (from the chapter)
If your product disappeared tomorrow, what would customers do that afternoon? If the answer is "shrug and revert," demand is aspirational, not real.

## Common failure patterns
1. **Solution in search of a problem** — the founder describes the product when asked about the problem. Force problem-only language for the whole session.
2. **Self-referential market** — "I have this problem" with no evidence others do at paying intensity.
3. **Vitamin framing** — improvement offered where the buyer needs pain removal. Vitamins sell on habit and brand (expensive); painkillers sell on urgency.
4. **Trigger blindness** — no answer to "what event makes someone go looking for this?" No trigger, no sales motion.

## Output the agent should produce
The duct-tape paragraph, pain-triangle legs with numbers, red/green tally, the three niche descriptions with the chosen one, and a go/no-go recommendation with reasons.
