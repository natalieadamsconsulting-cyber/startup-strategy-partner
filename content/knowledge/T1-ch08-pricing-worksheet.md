# Pricing Worksheet (expanded)

**Source:** The Founder's Field Manual, Chapter 8 — Pricing: The Hidden Super-Lever
**Agent use:** When a subscriber asks what to charge, hasn't raised prices in over a year, or is competing on price. Premise: pricing is the highest-leverage, least-tested decision in most startups — and almost every founder underprices.

## Step 1 — Name your fear
The chapter identifies four founder pricing fears. Ask which applies; the one the founder resists naming is usually the one setting the price. Underpricing is an emotional decision wearing a strategic costume.

## Step 2 — Run the value calculation (value-based pricing framework)
1. Customer's annual gain from the product, in dollars or hours
2. Realistic probability they capture that gain
3. Value of the next-best alternative
4. Ceiling price = (gain × probability) − next-best alternative

Compare the ceiling to the current price. **Same order of magnitude?** Most founders discover they are charging 5–10% of delivered value; 10–30% capture is a defensible target for B2B.

*Agent walk-through:* make the founder produce actual numbers. "My product saves an ops manager 6 hours/week × $50/hour × 50 weeks = $15,000/year. At 70% capture probability and a $3,000 next-best tool, ceiling ≈ $7,500/year. Charging $588/year is a 92% discount."

## Step 3 — Pick a pricing model
Match the business to one of the chapter's five archetypes (flat subscription, per-seat, usage-based, tiered, value/outcome-based). Ask: which are you using, which do you fit, and what changes if you switch? Misfit between model and value metric causes silent churn and capped expansion.

## Step 4 — Run one price test
Pick one of the chapter's three tests and commit to running it this quarter. **Decide in advance** what conversion-rate change would make the new price permanent. A price rise that cuts conversion 15% but lifts price 50% is a win — do the arithmetic before reacting to the anecdotes.

## Common failure patterns
1. **Cost-plus reflex** — pricing from your costs instead of customer value.
2. **Competitor anchoring** — pricing 20% below the incumbent, importing their model's flaws.
3. **Grandfathering forever** — never repricing existing customers; the base becomes an anchor dragging the whole book of business.
4. **One-price-fits-all** — no tiers means overcharging small customers and drastically undercharging large ones.

## Output the agent should produce
The value calculation with real numbers, the recommended model and why, one committed price test with a decision threshold and a date.
