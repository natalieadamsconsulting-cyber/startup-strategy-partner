# Field Notes: Blitzscaling (Reid Hoffman & Chris Yeh)

**Type:** Original summary — ideas, not text. Written for The Founder's Field Manual AI Agent.
**Agent use:** When a founder with proven product-market fit asks about growth pace, raising a big round, or "should we grow faster?" This is the counterweight to lean thinking — the agent should apply it *only after* validation, never before. Cross-reference: scaling worksheet (Ch 15), funding worksheet (T1-ch09).

## Core ideas
1. **Definition:** blitzscaling is prioritizing speed over efficiency *in an environment of uncertainty*, accepting waste and risk to reach critical scale before competitors. It is rational only when the market is winner-take-most (strong network effects or first-scaler advantages) and the prize justifies the burn.
2. **When it applies — the three tests:** (a) a big new opportunity where market size and margins make the prize enormous; (b) first-scaler advantage — whoever reaches scale first locks in customers, talent, and capital; (c) competition — if a rival is blitzscaling and you aren't, efficiency is how you lose politely. If these don't hold, classic efficient growth wins.
3. **Five stages by headcount metaphor:** family (1s) → tribe (10s) → village (100s) → city (1,000s) → nation (10,000s). Each transition breaks management practices that worked at the previous stage — the same insight as the Field Manual's Chapter 15, arrived at independently.
4. **Counterintuitive rules:** embrace chaos; hire "good enough now" over "perfect later"; tolerate bad management temporarily; launch products that embarrass you; let fires burn — triage which problems to ignore. These rules are *stage-conditional*, not general wisdom.
5. **What kills blitzscalers:** running out of cash mid-sprint (the model requires continuous capital access), scaling the org slower than the business, and scaling before product-market fit — the most common and most fatal misapplication.

## Where it aligns with the Field Manual
Chapter 15 (Scaling Without Breaking) is deliberately more conservative: it assumes most readers are *not* in winner-take-most markets. The agent should frame blitzscaling as a special regime: the Field Manual's operational discipline is the default; blitzscaling is the exception requiring affirmative evidence.

## Agent watch-fors — the qualification gate
Before endorsing any speed-over-efficiency move, require yes to all three:
1. Is retention strong enough that growth compounds instead of leaking? (If not: fix the bucket first.)
2. Is there a genuine first-scaler advantage — network effects, data moats, marketplace liquidity? (Most B2B SaaS: no.)
3. Is capital reliably available for the next 18–24 months of planned losses?

Founders cite blitzscaling to justify burn in markets with no winner-take-most dynamics — the agent's job is to name that. Growth at 3x with 130% burn multiple in a fragmented market is not blitzscaling; it is subsidized market share that churns when the subsidy stops.
