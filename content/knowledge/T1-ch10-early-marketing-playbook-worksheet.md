# Early Marketing Playbook Worksheet (expanded)

**Source:** The Founder's Field Manual, Chapter 10 — Marketing When No One Knows You Exist
**Agent use:** When a subscriber has a product but no distribution, is "posting on LinkedIn and hoping," or is spreading budget across five channels. Output: a one-page marketing plan they will actually use.

## Step 1 — Name your ideal early customer
Job title, behavior, pain points, industry, where they spend attention. **Test:** specific enough that someone outside the company could identify five real people who fit.

## Step 2 — Name the problem you solve — in their words
If you can't describe it in the customer's words, you haven't had enough customer conversations (return to T1-ch03). *Agent check:* ask for two verbatim quotes from real conversations. Marketing copy written from quotes converts; copy written from positioning meetings doesn't.

## Step 3 — Write your core message
"We help [who] do [what] so they can [outcome]." Read aloud; over ten seconds → rewrite. *Agent tests:* Would the named customer from Step 1 recognize themselves? Does the outcome clause name something they already want, or something they must be educated to want? (The second is a much more expensive company to build.)

## Step 4 — Pick your channel, and be honest why
Options: content, social (LinkedIn/X), product-led growth, cold outreach, community, PR, founder-led sales. Why this one — where do customers actually spend attention? **Commit to a full quarter before evaluating.**

*Bullseye discipline (from the chapter, after Weinberg & Mares):* test 6–8 channels with small cheap experiments, then concentrate on the one or two that show signal. The commitment rule applies to the *chosen* channel, not the tests. Early-stage default: personal outreach to a hyper-targeted list beats templated blasts — and beats every scalable channel until ~the first 50 customers.

*Channel-fit heuristics the agent can apply:* high ACV + identifiable buyers → founder-led outbound; product with fast time-to-value + low price → PLG; buyers who research before buying → content/SEO (12-month patience required); buyers clustered in communities → community-first. PR flatters and rarely converts; treat it as garnish.

## Step 5 — Define success in 30 days
One or two simple, measurable goals — leading indicators (qualified conversations started, demos booked), not lagging ones (revenue) at this stage.

## Common failure patterns
1. **Channel hopping** — three weeks per channel, concluding "nothing works." Nothing shows signal in three weeks.
2. **Automating before it works manually** — sequences and tooling scaling a message that has never closed anyone live.
3. **Speaking company language** — "AI-powered workflow orchestration" to a buyer who says "I waste Fridays chasing invoices."
4. **Delegating too early** — hiring a marketer before founder-led motion has produced one repeatable path to a customer.

## Output the agent should produce
The one-page plan: customer, problem in their words (with quotes), ten-second message, chosen channel with the honest why, the quarter commitment, and the 30-day goals.
