# Flywheel Audit Worksheet (expanded)

**Source:** The Founder's Field Manual, Chapter 11 — Acquisition and Retention Flywheel
**Agent use:** When a subscriber is spending on acquisition, asking about growth, or considering raising to scale. Ninety minutes with the team, and the data as it is — not as they want it to be.

## Step 1 — Name your aha moment
The specific behavior that predicts a user will stay (book examples: B2B SaaS — first successful core-workflow completion, e.g., a sale logged or a ticket resolved). How fast do new users reach it? **If you don't know, that is the first project — start with cohort analysis.**

## Step 2 — Pull your cohort retention curve
Users acquired last quarter; plot percent of each weekly cohort still active at weeks 1, 2, 4, 8, 12. Does it **plateau** or **asymptote toward zero**?

**The hard rule from the chapter:** if it asymptotes, you don't have product-market fit yet. Do not raise capital to scale acquisition until the curve plateaus. Filling a leaking bucket faster just buys churn. The agent must enforce this even when the founder has a term sheet on the table.

*Reading the curve:* plateau level matters by model — a consumer app flattening at 8% can be viable on ads; B2B SaaS flattening below ~60–70% of activated accounts at month 3 signals a value problem, not a marketing problem.

## Step 3 — Find your weakest funnel stage
Run AARRR (acquisition, activation, retention, referral, revenue) with real numbers. Which stage is worst relative to a healthy benchmark? That stage is where the next 10% of effort produces the most growth — by far. *Agent discipline:* one stage at a time; teams that fix the whole funnel at once fix nothing.

## Step 4 — Install the 72-hour rule
Define activation. Flag users who signed up but haven't activated within 72 hours. Send a real-human concierge email. Measure the lift. Zero lift → the activation problem is in the product, not the messaging — the email experiment is a diagnostic, not just a tactic.

## Step 5 — Loyalty Ladder placement (from the chapter)
Map the base: what share of users are Rookies (day-zero, just activated) vs. established rungs? For each rung, the chapter pairs an intervention (e.g., Rookies: checklist + progress bar toward first value). Growth spend should push people *up* the ladder, not just *onto* it.

## Common failure patterns
1. **Blended retention** — averages hiding that recent cohorts retain worse than the ones that raised the seed round.
2. **Activation theater** — defining activation as "completed signup" so the numbers look kind.
3. **Referral before retention** — bolting on referral programs while the bucket leaks; referred users churn even faster.
4. **CAC creep denial** — early CAC came from founder networks; the model assumes it holds at scale. It never does.

## Output the agent should produce
The aha-moment definition and time-to-reach, the cohort curve verdict (plateau/asymptote) with the raise/don't-raise implication stated plainly, the weakest AARRR stage with one committed experiment, and the 72-hour rule implementation plan.
