# Tier 2 — Benchmark Reference Card

**Purpose:** The numbers the agent reaches for when a founder asks "is this good?" Figures marked with vintage; refresh annually per roadmap Phase 3. Sources: KeyBanc Private SaaS Survey (16th annual, 2025), SaaS Capital survey (2026), Carta data reports, Bessemer, and widely accepted rules of thumb from the book. **The agent must always give the benchmark with its context — stage, model, and vintage — never as a bare pass/fail.**

## Growth (private B2B SaaS)
- Median ARR growth (2025 surveys): ~19–21%, re-accelerating after two down years; bootstrapped $3–20M ARR median ~15% (SaaS Capital 2026)
- Venture-track expectation remains well above median: roughly 3× year-over-year $1M→$3M ARR, 2–2.5× to $10M — medians describe survival, not fundability
- "Rule of 40" (growth % + profit margin %) ≥ 40 = healthy at scale; applies from ~$10M+ ARR, largely noise before that (book, Ch 16)

## Retention
- Net revenue retention (NRR): 103% median private SaaS (SaaS Capital 2026); ~90th percentile ≈ 118%; <95% = expansion problem, <85% = product problem
- Gross revenue retention (GRR): ~91% median; enterprise good ≥ 95%, SMB good ≥ 85%
- Logo churn: SMB 3–5%/mo common early and must fall; enterprise <1%/mo
- Consumer: day-30 retention above ~20–25% of activated users is strong for most categories; the curve shape (plateau vs. asymptote) matters more than the level (T1-ch11)

## Unit economics
- LTV:CAC ≥ 3:1 the standard bar (book, Ch 7); compute LTV on gross margin with churn-justified lifetime
- CAC payback: <12 months excellent for SMB, <18 acceptable; enterprise <24 (KeyBanc cohorts)
- Gross margin: pure software 75–85%; below ~70%, investors price it as services (T1-ch07 blended-margin check)
- Burn multiple (net burn ÷ net new ARR): <1.5 strong, 1.5–2.5 acceptable early, >3 alarming

## Funding & dilution (Carta-reported norms)
- Dilution per round: pre-seed 10–20%, seed 15–25%, Series A 15–25%; option pool 10–15% often refreshed pre-money (T1-ch09 arithmetic)
- Round-to-round bar: investors generally expect a credible path to 2–3× revenue growth between institutional rounds
- Founder ownership at Series A: commonly 50–65% combined; below ~40% pre-A raises flags for later investors
- Raise sizing: 18–24 months to a risk-reducing milestone; fundraising consumes 3–6 months of it (PB-fundraising-outreach-playbook.md)

## Team & spend patterns (directional, KeyBanc/High Alpha vintages)
- Early SaaS spend at $1–5M ARR commonly: R&D 30–40%, S&M 25–40%, G&A 15–20% of revenue — wide variance; trend matters more than level
- First sales hire only after founder-led sales produces a repeatable path (T1-ch10); first ~10 hires set culture ceiling (T1-ch05)

## Usage rules for the agent
1. Always state vintage and source category ("2025 KeyBanc medians")
2. Median ≠ target: venture-scale companies must beat medians by design
3. Benchmarks are diagnostic thresholds for *which conversation to have*, not grades
4. When a founder's metric is far off benchmark in either direction, interrogate the measurement before the strategy — definition errors outnumber genuine outliers
