# Validation Plan Worksheet (expanded)

**Source:** The Founder's Field Manual, Chapter 3 — Validation Before Code
**Agent use:** When a subscriber has an idea but hasn't validated it, or says "people say they love it" without payment or behavioral evidence. Design the conversations before having them.

## Step 1 — Name what you are testing
Write the single most dangerous assumption the business depends on, as one plain-English sentence. Not "users will love it." Closer to: "small-firm financial advisors will pay $400/month to consolidate their compliance feeds."

*Agent check:* the sentence must contain a specific customer, a specific behavior (ideally payment), and a specific magnitude. If any is missing, keep sharpening before proceeding.

## Step 2 — Pick your five conversations
Five real people, by name, by company, with email addresses, in writing:
1. The person who lives inside the problem day to day
2. The person who pays for the current solution
3. The person recently promoted into the problem
4. The person who tried to solve it once and gave up
5. The person who would lose the most if you succeeded

**Rule:** if the founder cannot name the five, that is the project. Build the list first.

## Step 3 — Write three polite-lie-proof questions
Replace opinion questions with behavior questions.
- Bad: "Would you use this?"
- Good: "When was the last time you had this problem, and what did you do about it?"

Behavior is harder to fake than opinion. Cross-reference: The Mom Test field notes (T3-mom-test.md) for the full question-design method.

## Signal-strength ladder (agent should rank evidence)
From weakest to strongest: compliments → detailed complaints about the current solution → time spent (a workaround they built) → reputation spent (an intro to their boss) → money spent (a paid pilot, a deposit, a signed LOI). The agent should push every founder at least one rung above where they are.

## Common failure patterns
1. **Pitching in the interview** — the founder demos instead of listening. Rule: describe the problem space, never the product, in the first five conversations.
2. **Friendly-sample bias** — five conversations with friends who all said yes. Require at least three strangers.
3. **Infinite validation** — 40 interviews and no decision. Set the kill/commit criteria *before* the conversations: "If X of 5 describe the problem unprompted and Y have paid for a workaround, I build."

## Output the agent should produce
A written validation plan: the dangerous assumption, the five named people, the three questions, the pre-committed kill/commit thresholds, and a two-week deadline.
