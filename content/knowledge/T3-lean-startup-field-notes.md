# Field Notes: The Lean Startup (Eric Ries)

**Type:** Original summary — ideas, not text. Written for The Founder's Field Manual AI Agent.
**Agent use:** When a founder mentions lean startup, pivots, or build-measure-learn, or is over-building. Cross-reference: MVP worksheet (T1-ch06), validation worksheet (T1-ch03).

## Core ideas
1. **A startup is an experiment factory.** Its product is validated learning about what customers actually want, produced under extreme uncertainty. Everything else — code, headcount, press — is inventory.
2. **Build–Measure–Learn.** The loop runs backward in planning: decide what you need to learn, determine what to measure to learn it, then build the smallest thing that produces the measurement. Founders who run it forward (build first) get the loop's cost without its information.
3. **MVP.** The smallest artifact that completes one loop. Ries's canonical examples: Dropbox's demo video (measured signups, not sync quality) and Zappos' founder photographing shoes in stores and buying them retail per order.
4. **Validated learning vs. vanity metrics.** Learning counts only when demonstrated by customer behavior change measured against a baseline — cohort metrics, split tests. Cumulative signup charts are vanity.
5. **Innovation accounting.** Establish baseline → tune the engine toward the ideal → after honest effort, pivot or persevere. The discipline is scheduling the pivot-or-persevere meeting *in advance*, so the decision isn't perpetually deferred.
6. **Pivot taxonomy.** A pivot changes one element of strategy while keeping the vision: zoom-in (feature becomes product), customer-segment, platform, value-capture, channel, engine-of-growth. Naming the type prevents the "slow death by many small pivots" pattern.
7. **Engines of growth.** Sticky (retention-driven), viral (users recruit users as a side effect), paid (margin funds acquisition). Pick one to tune at a time; each implies different metrics.

## Where it aligns with the Field Manual
Chapter 3's validation-before-code and Chapter 6's "build to learn, not to launch" are lean-startup thinking with sharper teeth: the Field Manual demands named testers, pre-committed thresholds, and payment-grade evidence, where Ries stays more abstract.

## Where the agent should push back on lean orthodoxy
- **Lean ≠ cheap forever.** Once the dangerous question is answered yes, under-investing becomes the new risk. Blitzscaling notes (T3-blitzscaling.md) cover the opposite regime.
- **Some products can't MVP small.** Deep tech, regulated fintech, medical: the "minimum" is large. There the MVP logic applies to *risk sequencing*, not product size.
- **Pivot inflation.** Founders use "pivot" to dignify drift. A pivot requires evidence from the last experiment; drift is a pivot without a baseline.
