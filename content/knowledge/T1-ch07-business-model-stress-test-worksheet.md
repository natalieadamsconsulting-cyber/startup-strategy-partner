# Business Model Stress-Test Worksheet (expanded)

**Source:** The Founder's Field Manual, Chapter 7 — Designing a Scalable Business Model
**Agent use:** When a subscriber asks "is my business model any good?", is preparing to raise, or shows revenue growing while margins shrink. One hour, in writing, brutal honesty.

## Step 1 — The leverage test
Three questions: Can you serve 10 customers with the resources of 1? Can the team handle 10× workload with 2× effort? Can technology replace manual work as you grow? Count the honest yeses.

*Scoring:* 3 yes → structurally scalable; 2 → scalable with named fixes; 0–1 → a services business wearing a software costume. That can be a fine business — but it changes the funding conversation entirely (see T1-ch09).

## Step 2 — Work your canvas
Fill the nine Business Model Canvas boxes for your company, then for the two companies in your space whose models you most respect. Where do your boxes differ? Are the differences your edge — or your problem?

*Agent facilitation:* do value proposition, revenue streams, and cost structure first; they expose the most. For each difference, ask "is this a deliberate bet or an accident of history?" Canvas reference: Strategyzer (cited in the book).

## Step 3 — Run your unit economics
CAC, LTV, payback period — with real numbers. LTV:CAC below 3:1 → what part of the model must change?

*Agent math discipline:* CAC must be fully loaded (sales salaries, tooling, founder time approximated — not just ad spend). LTV must use gross margin, not revenue, and a churn-justified lifetime. Under ~12 months of data, treat LTV as an estimate with wide error bars and say so. Payback >18 months in SMB or >24 in enterprise is a financing problem the founder must plan for.

## Step 4 — Check for the non-scalable trap
Is headcount growing in line with customer count? Is gross margin improving or degrading with scale? What percent of senior-team time goes to customer-specific work? Degrading margin + linear headcount + >20% custom work = the trap, regardless of topline growth.

## Step 5 — Pre-commit to one change
One model change, decided now, with a date — not a list. The agent should refuse to let the session end with "interesting, we'll think about it."

## Common failure patterns
1. **Blended-margin hiding** — services revenue mixed into "software" margins. Split the lines.
2. **LTV fantasy** — 5-year lifetimes asserted from 8 months of history.
3. **Copying the canvas of a company at a different stage** — Salesforce's model boxes are earned, not adopted.
4. **Scalability someday** — "we'll automate it later" for work that is being custom-built deal by deal today. Later never comes without a forcing decision.

## Output the agent should produce
Leverage score with evidence, the canvas comparison with each difference tagged edge/problem/accident, the unit-economics table with data-confidence notes, the trap check, and the one dated change. Cross-reference: T3-seven-powers-field-notes.md for which power the model earns as it scales.
