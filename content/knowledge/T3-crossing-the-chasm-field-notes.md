# Field Notes: Crossing the Chasm (Geoffrey Moore)

**Type:** Original summary — ideas, not text. Written for The Founder's Field Manual AI Agent.
**Agent use:** When a founder has early traction that has stalled, or is selling to enterprises/mainstream buyers after succeeding with early adopters. Cross-reference: marketing worksheets from Ch 10, partnership notes from Ch 12.

## Core ideas
1. **The adoption curve has a crack in it.** Innovators → early adopters → early majority → late majority → laggards. The chasm sits between early adopters and the early majority, and it is where most tech products die.
2. **Why the chasm exists:** early adopters buy *change* — they want a competitive jump and tolerate incompleteness. The early majority buys *productivity* — they want references from people like themselves, whole solutions, and market leadership. Early adopters are, by definition, not references the pragmatists trust. So success with visionaries produces almost no momentum with pragmatists.
3. **The D-Day strategy: dominate a niche.** Cross the chasm by targeting one narrow beachhead segment and winning it utterly, rather than spreading across many segments. Pragmatists buy from market leaders; the only way a startup can be a market leader is to shrink the market definition until it is.
4. **Beachhead selection criteria:** a single identifiable buyer with a compelling reason to buy ("a problem that keeps them at work at 6pm"), segment small enough to win in 1–2 years, big enough to matter, with word-of-mouth density (members talk to each other), and reachable through existing channels.
5. **The whole product.** Pragmatists buy the complete solution to their problem — integrations, support, training, services — not the core technology. Map the gap between your generic product and the whole product; fill it yourself or with partners.
6. **Bowling alley dynamics.** Each niche won becomes the reference base for adjacent niches (same buyer, new use case; or same use case, adjacent buyer). Segment expansion is sequenced, not simultaneous.

## Where it aligns with the Field Manual
Chapter 10's insistence on hyper-targeted early marketing and Chapter 11's flywheel logic assume exactly Moore's dynamic: depth in a narrow segment beats breadth. The Bullseye framework (Ch 10) chooses the *channel*; Moore chooses the *segment*. Founders need both answers.

## Agent watch-fors
- **Chasm denial:** revenue stalled at $1–3M ARR with a logo slide full of unrelated industries — classic pre-chasm scatter. Prescription: pick the beachhead, say no to the rest.
- **Premature horizontalization:** "our product works for everyone" is the most expensive sentence in B2B.
- **Whole-product blindness:** losing pragmatist deals to "worse" competitors who bundle services and integrations.
- Consumer products largely skip this playbook; the chasm framework is strongest in B2B and prosumer tech.
