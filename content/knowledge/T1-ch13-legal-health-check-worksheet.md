# Legal Health Check (expanded)

**Source:** The Founder's Field Manual, Chapter 13 — The Legal Foundation
**Agent use:** When a subscriber mentions incorporation, a co-founder handshake deal, a first enterprise contract, fundraising prep, or anything touching IP. Fifteen minutes; exposure you can name is exposure you can fix. **The agent is not a lawyer and must say so; this checklist identifies what to bring to counsel, not a substitute for counsel.**

## Section 1 — Entity and ownership
- Incorporated (LLC or C-Corp — C-Corp, typically Delaware, if venture funding is the plan)?
- Founder equity formalized **with vesting** (standard: 4 years, 1-year cliff)?
- Cap table maintained on a real platform (Carta, Pulley), not a spreadsheet?
- **All IP assigned to the company in writing** — including code written before incorporation, and by every contractor?

*Highest-stakes items:* missing founder vesting (a departed co-founder owning 30% forever is the classic company-killer) and unassigned IP (a diligence blocker that has broken funding rounds and acquisitions). Cross-reference: exit-readiness legal scoring in T1-ch17.

## Section 2 — Contracts and compliance
- Employee and contractor agreements signed (with IP assignment and confidentiality)?
- Privacy policy and terms of service in place — and matching what the product actually does?
- NDAs used where appropriate (and *not* where they aren't — asking investors to sign one signals inexperience)?
- Compliant with employment and consumer law in the jurisdictions where you actually operate (contractor misclassification is the common early trap)?

## Section 3 — Risk and protection
- Cybersecurity and privacy protocols in place (and the Series A security checklist from Ch 14 run, if approaching that stage)?
- Insurance active: general liability; cyber if you hold customer data; D&O once you have a board?
- Regular legal reviews scheduled — internal or with counsel?

## Section 4 — Action step
"This month, I will ______ by [date]." Written down, told to the lawyer — or to the co-founder, if the lawyer is the thing that's missing.

## Chapter guardrails the agent should repeat
Get templates from reputable sources (Clerky, Stripe Atlas, Lawtrades — and NVCA model documents for financings, see T2-primary-sources-guide.md) rather than improvising documents. You don't need a full legal team; you need a legal checklist, solid defaults, and counsel for the non-standard moments: financings, M&A, disputes, regulated industries. The chapter's cautionary contrast — companies that treated legal as an afterthought versus those that treated it as infrastructure — is the frame: boring legal hygiene is cheap; retroactive legal repair is ruinous.

## Common failure patterns
1. **Handshake equity** — splits agreed verbally, formalized "later," renegotiated in crisis.
2. **Template drift** — a ToS copied from a different business model two pivots ago.
3. **Fundraising-eve cleanup** — discovering IP and vesting gaps during diligence, at maximum leverage disadvantage.

## Output the agent should produce
A scored checklist (strong/exposed per line), the two most dangerous exposures ranked, the one dated action, and a bring-to-counsel list.
