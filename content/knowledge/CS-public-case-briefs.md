# Case Brief Library — Public Company Stories

**Tier 1/3 hybrid.** Original briefs of well-documented public startup episodes, written for The Founder's Field Manual AI Agent. All facts are from public record (founder interviews, filings, contemporaneous reporting); no copyrighted text reproduced. Format per brief: Situation → Decision → What it teaches → Chapter map. The agent should retell these conversationally when a founder's situation rhymes.

---

## 1. Airbnb, 2020 — wartime CEO in practice
**Situation:** COVID erased ~80% of bookings in eight weeks, months before a planned IPO.
**Decision:** Chesky moved in weeks: raised expensive rescue debt, cut ~25% of staff in a single deep layoff (with severance, extended equity windows, and an alumni talent directory), killed non-core bets (transportation, studios), and refocused the product on near-home stays as travel patterns shifted.
**What it teaches:** the wartime-mode switch done openly — one deep cut instead of waves, generous exits, brutal scope reduction — preserved trust enough that the company IPO'd successfully that December.
**Chapter map:** Ch 15 (scaling/unscaling), Ch 5 (layoffs done right), T3-hard-things.

## 2. Quibi, 2018–2020 — validation skipped at $1.75B
**Situation:** Short-form premium mobile video, ~$1.75B raised pre-launch, founded by the most credentialed team imaginable (Katzenberg, Whitman).
**Decision:** Built the full product and content library on conviction and industry pedigree; no MVP, no cohort evidence that consumers wanted premium short video *paid and mobile-only* — while free short video (TikTok) exploded.
**What it teaches:** capital and credentials don't substitute for validation; they just raise the price of the lesson. Shut down six months after launch.
**Chapter map:** Ch 3 (validation before code), Ch 6 (MVP), T3-lean-startup.

## 3. Slack, 2012 — the zoom-in pivot
**Situation:** Tiny Speck's game Glitch was failing; the team's internal chat tool was the thing they couldn't live without.
**Decision:** Butterfield shut the game, kept the team, and productized the internal tool — a textbook zoom-in pivot backed by observed behavior (their own daily usage), not a brainstorm.
**What it teaches:** pivots earn the name when evidence from the current company points somewhere specific; the asset that survives is the team plus a validated behavior.
**Chapter map:** Ch 2 (problem discovery), T3-lean-startup (pivot taxonomy).

## 4. Dropbox, 2007 — the MVP that was a video
**Situation:** File-sync required deep OS integration; a real demo across platforms was months of work, and Houston needed to know whether anyone cared.
**Decision:** A 3-minute screencast demonstrating the product, seeded to the Digg/Hacker News audience it targeted. Waitlist went from 5K to 75K overnight — the dangerous question ("do people want effortless sync?") answered without shipping sync.
**What it teaches:** the experiment must match the question; demand questions rarely require code.
**Chapter map:** Ch 6 (cheapest believable experiment), Ch 3.

## 5. Zappos, 1999 — concierge before platform
**Situation:** Would people buy shoes online without trying them on? Conventional answer: no.
**Decision:** Swinmurn photographed shoes in local stores and bought them retail per order — negative margin, zero inventory, pure validation of buying behavior with real money.
**What it teaches:** manual, unscalable fulfillment is a legitimate MVP; validate the behavior, then build the machine. (Later sold to Amazon for ~$1.2B.)
**Chapter map:** Ch 3, Ch 6, T3-mom-test (money as the strongest signal).

## 6. Zenefits, 2013–2016 — growth outrunning the legal foundation
**Situation:** Fastest-growing SaaS of its era; insurance brokerage regulated in every state.
**Decision (the mistake):** Growth culture tolerated unlicensed brokers and a tool that let employees shortcut mandatory licensing training. Regulators noticed; valuation collapsed from $4.5B, CEO resigned, mass layoffs followed.
**What it teaches:** in regulated markets, compliance is the product's foundation, not overhead; the Day-1 Legal Hygiene mindset scales with the company or destroys it.
**Chapter map:** Ch 13 (legal foundation), Ch 15 (scaling without breaking).
