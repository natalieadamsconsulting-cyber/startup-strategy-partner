# Playbook: Hiring Scorecard & Interview Kit

**Tier 1 — original playbook.** Extends Ch 5 (Hiring Your First Ten) and the T1-ch05 worksheet, which produces the role definition this playbook operationalizes.
**Agent use:** Turn a completed role definition into a full interview process. The principle: decide what evidence would prove the candidate can deliver the 90-day outcomes, then design each interview to collect specific evidence — not impressions.

## The scorecard (one page per role, written before any interview)
1. **Mission of the role** — the leverage sentence from T1-ch05
2. **90-day outcomes** — 3, each measurable or artifact-producing
3. **Competencies** — 4–6 max, each with a behavioral definition ("ownership: has personally carried a project through failure and rework without being managed into it")
4. **Culture screens** — the fire-for values from T1-ch04, phrased as observable behavior
5. **Red flags** — from T1-ch05 Step 3, each paired with its probe question

Every interviewer scores 1–4 per assigned competency (4-point scale forces a lean; 3-point scales collect fence-sitting). Scores submitted in writing **before** any group discussion — the first strong opinion voiced otherwise anchors everyone.

## Interview loop design (early-stage appropriate: 3–4 sessions, ~1 week)
- **Screen (30 min, founder):** motivation, trajectory, two facts checked against the résumé. Kill fast and kindly.
- **Deep-dive (60–90 min):** chronological walk through the last 2–3 roles. For each: what were you hired to do, what did you actually ship, what would your manager say was your weakness, why did you leave? Patterns across roles are the signal; single stories are rehearsed.
- **Work session (60–90 min, paid if substantial):** a real, bounded problem from the company's actual backlog. Assess how they think, ask, and handle pushback — not whether they land the "right" answer. Never use spec work you intend to ship.
- **Values + sell (founder or co-founder):** the red-flag probes from the scorecard, then an honest sell including what's broken. Candidates who lean in when told the truth stay; candidates sold a dream churn at month six, at maximum cost.

## Reference calls — the most skipped, highest-yield step
Two references minimum, at least one manager, at least one *off-list* (a former colleague found through the network). Script: "Would you hire them again — for this specific role as I've described it?"; "They rank in which decile of people you've managed?"; "What support did they need to succeed?" Listen for enthusiasm gaps; polite references are negative references.

## Decision protocol
Scores in writing → discussion → founder decides. Rules: no hire on a split where the deep-dive interviewer is the doubter; "smart but violates a culture screen" is a no (the taxonomy in T3-hard-things-field-notes.md); a 60-day scorecard review against the 90-day outcomes is scheduled *at offer time*, so the difficult conversation, if needed, has a natural venue.

## Agent watch-fors
- Interviews that are unstructured conversations — every session must own named competencies.
- The "we just clicked" hire; chemistry predicts nothing beyond the interview.
- Outcomes written after meeting a candidate they want to justify.
- Skipping references under time pressure — the step that catches what interviews structurally cannot.

## Output the agent should produce
The completed one-page scorecard, the loop plan with competency assignments per interviewer, the deep-dive and red-flag question sets, and the reference script.
