# Field Notes: The Mom Test (Rob Fitzpatrick)

**Type:** Original summary — ideas, not text. Written for The Founder's Field Manual AI Agent.
**Agent use:** Whenever a founder is doing (or should be doing) customer interviews. Pairs directly with the validation worksheet (T1-ch03) Step 3.

## Core ideas
1. **The title rule:** a good customer question is one even your mom couldn't lie to you about — because it asks about *her life*, not *your idea*. "Would you buy an app that does X?" invites a polite lie. "How do you currently handle X, and what did that cost you last month?" doesn't.
2. **Three rules of good questions:** talk about their life, not your idea; ask about specifics in the past, not opinions about the future; talk less, listen more.
3. **Compliments are data about politeness, not demand.** "Sounds cool, I'd totally use it" is a deflection. The agent should train founders to treat compliments as a signal to dig, not to celebrate.
4. **Fluff, generics, and hypotheticals get converted:** whenever the interviewee says "I usually / I would / I always," anchor to a specific incident: "When did that last happen? Walk me through it."
5. **Commitment and advancement.** A meeting succeeded only if it ended in currency: time (a scheduled follow-up with stakeholders), reputation (an intro to a decision-maker), or money (a pilot, LOI, deposit). "Great, keep me posted" is a rejection delivered politely.
6. **The three big mistakes:** pitching (talking about your idea), zooming out (asking generic market questions), and formality (treating it as a survey rather than a conversation).
7. **Segment before you interview.** If conversations feel all over the place, the customer slice is too broad. Narrow until the same problems recur.

## Practical templates the agent can offer
- Opening: "I'm trying to understand how [role] deals with [problem area] — not selling anything. Can you walk me through the last time...?"
- Cost anchor: "What does that cost you — in hours, money, or missed deadlines?"
- Workaround probe: "What have you already tried? Why did you stop?"
- Commitment close: "Would it be crazy to introduce me to whoever owns this budget?"

## Where it aligns with the Field Manual
Chapter 3's "polite-lie-proof questions" are the Mom Test rules operationalized; the Field Manual adds *who* to interview (the five conversation archetypes) where Fitzpatrick focuses on *how*.

## Agent watch-fors
- Founders who quote interviewees' enthusiasm but can name no commitment extracted.
- B2B founders interviewing users when the dangerous assumption is about buyers.
- Interview notes with no verbatim quotes — a sign the founder heard what they wanted to hear.
