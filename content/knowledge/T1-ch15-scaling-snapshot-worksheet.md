# Scaling Snapshot Worksheet (expanded)

**Source:** The Founder's Field Manual, Chapter 15 — Scaling Without Breaking
**Agent use:** When a subscriber's company is growing fast and things are creaking — missed handoffs, founder bottlenecks, quality slips — or when they ask "are we ready to scale?" Twenty minutes: map where you are and where you need to grow.

## Step 1 — What is working, and what breaks under pressure?
Name one workflow that is efficient and consistent, and one that breaks under pressure. **The stress question:** if volume doubled tomorrow, which step in either workflow breaks first?

*Agent probe:* "breaks first" should be a specific step and a specific person ("Maria manually approves every refund"), not a department. Single-person dependencies are the most common first fracture.

## Step 2 — Where do you need people?
Which roles are overworked? What is on the founder's plate that shouldn't be? Which next hire most likely unblocks the company in the next ninety days? Cross-reference T1-ch05 for the role scorecard once identified.

## Step 3 — Score your four pillars
**Process, People, Technology, Metrics — each 1–5.** Pick the lowest score: that is next quarter's focus. One pillar per quarter; the chapter's point is that scaling breaks companies through the *weakest* pillar, not the average one.

*Calibration:* 1 = tribal knowledge, heroics, no documentation; 3 = documented but bypassed under pressure; 5 = the system works when the founder is on holiday. Most scaling-stage companies honestly score 2–3 everywhere — that's normal, not alarming.

## Step 4 — The Founder Mirror
From the chapter's founder-job table: which row *should* you be on today, and which row are you *actually* operating in? What is the gap and the first move to close it?

*The pattern behind the question:* founders default to the work that built the company (selling, shipping, firefighting) long after the job has become designing the machine that does those things. The chapter's Hard Pattern (introduced in Ch 5) applies to the founder too: the operating mode that carried one stage cannot carry the next. The fix is unglamorous — one responsibility formally handed off at a time, with authority, not just tasks.

## Org structure check (from the chapter)
If team structure is on the table: functional works until cross-team handoffs dominate; matrix structures multiply reporting lines and can slow a competent team by overstructuring. Don't adopt a bigger company's org chart as a costume.

## Common failure patterns
1. **Scaling by heroics** — growth absorbed by people working harder; it compounds until the best people leave.
2. **Process theater** — process added where trust is missing rather than where volume demands it; the inverse error of no process at all.
3. **Offsite substitution** — treating the scaling problem as a strategy or morale problem. It's usually the difficult, unglamorous work of fixing the weakest pillar (the chapter is explicit on this).
4. **Founder-as-bottleneck denial** — every decision still routes through one calendar; the org chart says otherwise, the Slack history doesn't. Run the Operational Tune-Up Checklist from the chapter as the deeper follow-up.

## Output the agent should produce
The two named workflows with the break-first step, the 90-day unblocking hire, the four pillar scores with next quarter's single focus, the Founder Mirror gap with the first handoff, and a doubled-volume stress summary. Offer a re-score in 90 days.
