# Field Notes: The Hard Thing About Hard Things (Ben Horowitz)

**Type:** Original summary — ideas, not text. Written for The Founder's Field Manual AI Agent.
**Agent use:** When a founder is in crisis — layoffs, firing an executive, running out of cash, losing conviction — or asks about the psychological side of the job. This is the agent's reference for the moments no framework covers. Cross-reference: hiring worksheet (Ch 5), scaling notes (Ch 15).

## Core ideas
1. **The Struggle.** Horowitz names the psychological state every founder hits: the company is failing, everyone is looking at you, and there is no formula. His claim: the struggle is not a sign you're failing — it is the job. What separates outcomes is composure and continuing to make moves when there are no good ones.
2. **Peacetime CEO / wartime CEO.** Peacetime: expand the market, encourage creativity, tolerate deviation from plan. Wartime (existential threat): violate protocol, obsess over the single bullet in the chamber, demand precision. Most CEO advice is peacetime advice; applying it in wartime is fatal. The founder must know which mode the company is in — and switch openly.
3. **Tell it like it is.** Withholding bad news from employees fails because (a) they can handle it, (b) hiding it destroys trust, and (c) the people who can solve the problem need to know it exists. A culture that punishes bad news guarantees the CEO learns problems last.
4. **Take care of the people, the products, and the profits — in that order.** Being a good place to work matters most exactly when things go badly.
5. **Lead bullets.** When a competitor is beating you on product, there is no silver bullet — no repositioning or partnership trick. Only lead bullets: make a better product. The temptation to avoid the direct fight is a tell.
6. **Hire for strength, not lack of weakness.** Big-company executives fail at startups by default (wrong rhythm: they wait for work to come to them). Hire for the one thing you need world-class, tolerate the gaps, and manage them.
7. **Firing and layoffs done right:** train managers to deliver the message themselves, be honest that it's the company's failure not the employee's, do it once and deep rather than in waves, and address the survivors the same day.
8. **The smart-but-toxic taxonomy** — the heretic, the flake, the jerk: brilliant people whose net effect is negative. The CEO's failure mode is keeping them too long because of the brilliance.

## Where it aligns with the Field Manual
Chapter 5's "Hard" hiring patterns and Chapter 15's honest treatment of what breaks during scaling share Horowitz's core stance: most business books describe the sunny path; the value is in the dark parts. The Field Manual's story-backed format is the same bet.

## Agent watch-fors
- A founder describing an existential threat while operating in peacetime mode (consensus-seeking, long timelines). Name the mode mismatch.
- A founder who hasn't told the team how bad it is. The agent should ask: "What does your team believe about the situation, and how far is it from the truth?"
- Layoff plans sized to hit this quarter's number rather than sized once, deep enough to not repeat.
