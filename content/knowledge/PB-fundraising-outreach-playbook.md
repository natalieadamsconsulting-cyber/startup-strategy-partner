# Playbook: Fundraising Outreach & Process

**Tier 1 — original playbook.** Extends Ch 9 (Funding Your Startup). Prerequisite: the T1-ch09 worksheet completed — the founder knows the amount, the milestone, and the *because*.
**Agent use:** Walk the founder through running a fundraise as a designed process, not a series of coffee chats. Adapt all copy to the founder's voice and facts.

## Phase 1 — Pipeline before pitch (2–3 weeks)
Build a list of 40–60 investors, tiered: **A** (perfect thesis fit, dream partners), **B** (solid fit), **C** (practice). Qualify each: stage, check size, sector thesis, recent relevant deals, conflicts (portfolio competitors). **Sequence C-tier first** — the pitch improves for two weeks before an A-tier partner ever hears it. Track in a simple CRM: firm, partner, intro path, status, next action, notes.

**Warm paths rank:** portfolio founder intro > co-investor intro > direct-but-researched cold email > cold outreach via associates. Ask each intro-maker for one specific introduction, not "anyone who might be interested."

## Phase 2 — The forwardable email
The intro request email must be forwardable as-is:

> Subject: [Company] — [one-line traction hook]
> Hi [name] — we're building [what] for [who]. Since [date]: [2–3 sharpest proof points — revenue, growth rate, named logos, retention]. We're raising $[X] to [milestone]. Given [firm]'s work in [thesis area], [partner] seems like the right conversation. Could you forward this along? Deck attached.

Rules: under 120 words; numbers over adjectives; one named partner, not "your network"; no "stealth," no NDAs (see T1-ch13).

## Phase 3 — Running the process
- **Compress:** open all conversations within a 2–3 week window; velocity creates the scarcity that produces term sheets. A raise dragged over five months signals weakness regardless of the business.
- **Meeting cadence:** first partner meeting → data room only after genuine engagement → partner meeting → terms. Founder controls the pace by batching.
- **The weekly update** to engaged investors during the raise (3 bullets: progress, pipeline heat, timeline) keeps momentum without desperation.
- **Qualify ruthlessly:** "What would you need to see to lead?" after meeting two. Investors who can't answer are tourists; move them to the update list and stop spending live hours.

## Phase 4 — Terms and close
When a term sheet lands: 48–72 hours to inform other engaged firms ("we've received a term sheet; our timeline has moved up") — this is the only moment the founder holds full leverage. Take valuation *and* terms to counsel (liquidation preference above 1× non-participating, aggressive option-pool shuffle, and board-control asks are the usual traps — Venture Deals field notes per roadmap). The highest valuation from the wrong partner is expensive money.

## Agent watch-fors
- Founders "testing the waters" with A-tier firms unprepared — first impressions don't reset.
- Pipeline of 8 investors ("we know them already") — statistically a failed raise; conversion from first meeting to term sheet runs ~5–10%.
- Update emails that only appear when the founder needs something.
- Reading polite passes as encouragement; a pass with feedback is a pass.

## Output the agent should produce
The tiered pipeline table, the founder's forwardable email drafted in their facts, a 6-week process calendar, and the qualification script.
