# Partnership Planning Canvas (expanded)

**Source:** The Founder's Field Manual, Chapter 12 — Strategic Partnerships
**Agent use:** When a subscriber is excited about a potential partnership, has been approached by a bigger company, or asks "should we partner with X?" Excitement is the danger sign — partnerships are where early-stage time goes to die.

## Step 1 — What gap are you trying to fill?
Distribution? Technology? Co-marketing reach? Brand credibility? **Rule:** more than one answer = at least two partnerships to plan, not one. A single deal asked to fill multiple gaps fills none.

## Step 2 — What would the partner gain?
Specifically: in their P&L, their KPIs, or their roadmap. Mutual value is what makes partnerships last. If you cannot answer, do more discovery before any outreach. *Agent probe:* which named person inside the partner gets promoted if this succeeds? No name → no internal champion → no deal, whatever the LOI says.

## Step 3 — Which quadrant does this live in? (The Partnership Trap matrix)
Four quadrants: **Trap** (their gain, your distraction — where founders accidentally end up), **Lock-in** (value now, dependency later), **Waste of time** (mutual indifference dressed as synergy), **Sweet spot** (clear mutual value, bounded commitment — where the rare ones live).

*Trap tells:* the bigger party asks for exclusivity, custom product work, or long integration lead time before any revenue commitment; the meetings are with their "innovation" team rather than a P&L owner. Lock-in tells: >30% of revenue or roadmap through one partner; the chapter's large-cap examples show both directions — study them as illustrations, not templates.

## Step 4 — Who could you find them among?
Vendors, customers, advisors, peer founders — your existing orbit. If the only candidates are companies with no existing relationship, you are probably not ready for this partnership yet.

## Step 5 — Who will own the relationship?
If the answer is the founder, the deal is too expensive to sustain. Assign a partnership lead with clear authority *before* signing.

## Structural terms the agent should push for
A pilot with an expiry date before any long-term agreement; revenue or usage commitments, not marketing promises; no exclusivity without payment for it; a named executive sponsor on their side; and pre-agreed success metrics with a review date.

## Common failure patterns
1. **Logo hunting** — partnering for the press release. Announcements don't retain customers.
2. **The BD mirage** — six months of calls with a big company that was never going to move; their exploration costs them nothing and costs the startup its focus.
3. **Building to spec** — custom features for a partnership that dies, leaving orphan code.
4. **Exclusivity given away** — granted casually in an early draft because saying no felt risky.

## Output the agent should produce
The completed canvas: the single gap, the partner's specific gain with the named champion, the honest quadrant placement, the sourcing list from the existing orbit, the named owner, and — if it's a Trap or Waste of time — a polite exit script.
